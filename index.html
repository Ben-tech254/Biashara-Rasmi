Benefits

Seven doors that open when you go formal

  1. Bigger markets

    Supermarkets, exporters and government tenders. 30% of tenders are set aside for youth, women and people with disabilities.

  2. Loans

    Government funds like the Youth and Women Enterprise Funds, and banks, lend more to businesses they can verify.

  3. Grants

    Money you don’t pay back. Most grant programmes only accept registered businesses.

  4. Trust

    Customers, buyers and investors can look you up and see you’re real.

  5. Quality mark

    Get your product certified by KEBS. Supermarkets look for the mark, and small makers pay lower fees.

  6. Network

    Trade fairs, exhibitions and partners open up to you, at home and abroad.

  7. Protection

    Your business name is yours alone, and a limited company keeps your home safe from business debts.

The downside

What staying informal costs you

  • Shut out of big buyers

    No supermarkets, exporters or government tenders.

  • No real loans

    Banks and funds can’t verify you, so you stay small.

  • No grants

    Most programmes only fund registered businesses.

  • Fines and crackdowns

    No county permit means fines, closures or seized stock.

  • Your name isn’t safe

    Anyone can copy or register your business name first.

  • Hard to grow

    No contracts, no investors, no exhibitions.
