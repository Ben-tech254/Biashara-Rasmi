Steps

Go formal,
step by step.

Almost everything happens online through BRS on eCitizen, even from a phone.

  1. Pick your business type

    Sole ownerCheapest and fastest.
    PartnershipTwo or more owners.
    Company (Ltd)Best for investors, protects your assets.

  2. Get your KRA PIN

    Every owner and director needs one. Apply on iTax with your ID.

  3. Reserve your name on eCitizen

    Log in, open BRS and search up to three names. An approved name is held for 30 days.

    About KES 150

  4. Upload your documents

    • ID and KRA PIN
    • Photos
    • Forms CR1 and CR8
    • Nominal capital statement
    • Memorandum and Articles
    • Beneficial owners

    Names must match exactly everywhere. It’s the #1 cause of delays.

  5. Pay and get your certificate

    Pay on eCitizen. Sole owners wait a few days, companies up to about two weeks.

    Sole owner ~KES 950 · Company ~KES 10,000–10,650

  6. Register with KRA

    Get a business PIN and set up eTIMS invoicing. VAT only applies above KES 5 million turnover a year.

  7. Finish up locally

    Get your county single business permit. Hiring? Register with NSSF and SHIF too. Then file your annual returns.

Fees change. Confirm on eCitizen and KRA before you pay, and only pay through the official portal. Fees shown are 2026 estimates.

Hurdles

“I want to, but…”

Every founder has these worries. Each one has an answer.

“It costs too much.”

Start small. A sole proprietorship is about KES 950. Upgrade to a company when you’re ready. One grant or one big customer can repay it many times over.

“The paperwork is confusing.”

It’s a checklist. Follow the steps in order. Stuck? An accountant or company secretary can file for you, and everything is still yours.

“I’m afraid of tax.”

Registering isn’t a huge tax bill. VAT only starts above KES 5 million a year. Ask KRA or an accountant which tax option fits your size.

“I’m too small.”

Every big brand started small. You still run things your way, now with the power to sign bigger contracts and win grants.

“I don’t have the time or a smartphone.”

One evening can be enough. Gather your documents first, then fill in the forms in one go. A cyber café or a trusted accountant can help.

“I don’t know who to trust.”

Use only the official portal. Pay on eCitizen only. Beware of anyone who promises to “fast-track” you for cash.
